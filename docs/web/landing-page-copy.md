## Section 4: Solution (How It Works)

### Section Header

How context.help works

### Step 1: Curate your registry

Structure entities, taxonomies, edges, and safe summaries. Publish structured context that agents can query and resolve—not static docs they have to scrape.

*Icon suggestion: Plus/create icon*

### Step 2: Define policy and access

Your registry can be self-hosted or cloud-managed. **Systems sync the index—not full bodies—so you control distribution without vendor lock-in.** Set quotas and access rules to protect your work.

*Icon suggestion: Lock/shield icon*

### Step 3: Let systems pull JiT

**Agents pull specific entities, edges, or summaries—just what's needed for the task at hand.** Your context integrates into workflows, pipelines, and preference graphs—not inboxes.

*Icon suggestion: Network/connection icon*

## Section 5: Key Benefits

### Benefit 1: Systems pull what they need. Not everything you have.

No bulk replication. No scraping entire databases. Index-level sync means **agents get fresh context without you exposing everything upfront.**

### Benefit 2: You set the rules. Systems follow them.

Self-host your registry anywhere, or use context.help cloud. Either way, **you define who can access what, how much they can pull, and when—systems respect your rules by default.**

### Benefit 3: Traceable by design

**Every resolution carries receipts and provenance.**

Know where your context went, **which agents used it**, and what it contributed to. If a system misbehaves or a subscriber disputes usage, you have the receipts.

### Section Header

Why we're building this

### Body Copy

For years, knowledge workers have been forced to choose between two broken systems:

1. **Centralized platforms** (Notion, Confluence) that treat context like static files humans read
2. **Scattered tools** (Slack, wikis, email) where insights get buried and systems can't reach them

And just like those ecosystems, **you stay sovereign**. Your registry, your rules, your infrastructure (if you want it).

That's what we're building at context.help.

## Section 10: FAQ

### Is this for individuals or teams?

Both. Individuals can curate and publish registries. **Orgs subscribe and set the rules for how their systems can query your registry**—whether they self-host or use context.help cloud.

### Do I have to self-host?

Nope. You can use context.help cloud if you don't want to manage infrastructure. Self-hosting is an option for teams that need it—not a requirement.

### What if my context gets stolen or misused?

Index-first distribution means **systems get safe summaries by default—not full content**. You set quotas to prevent bulk scraping. And every resolution creates a receipt, so you can audit exactly who pulled what, when.

### Can agents integrate this with existing workflows?

**Yes. That's the point.** Registries expose resolver-native APIs. Agents can query, filter, and pull context based on task, policy, and preference graphs—not just dump everything into RAG.

### Can I migrate my existing knowledge base?

We're building import tools for common formats (Notion exports, markdown repos, etc.). More details coming as we get closer to launch.
